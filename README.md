### 🛑 GROUP 1: DOG BITE AND RABIES MANAGEMENT

#### 📍 Scenario 1
A 14-year-old boy is brought to a health post 30 minutes after being bitten by a stray dog on his right lower leg. The wound is bleeding slightly and there is visible dirt around the area.

**1. What is the first immediate action the nurse should take for this dog bite wound, and why?**
- [ ] A) Apply tight pressure and stitch it immediately to stop bleeding.
- [ ] B) Gently cleanse and irrigate the wound to remove dirt and reduce viral/bacterial load.
- [ ] C) Cover the wound immediately with a dry sterile dressing without cleaning.
- [ ] D) Apply traditional herbs or butter to soothe the skin.

**2. If the dog is suspected to have rabies, within how many hours should anti-rabies treatment ideally be initiated?**
- [ ] A) Within 24 to 48 hours.
- [ ] B) Only after the dog shows clear signs of dying.
- [ ] C) Within 72 hours.
- [ ] D) Immediately/as soon as possible before symptoms develop.

**3. Which specific vaccine must be given to this patient following the dog bite?**
- [ ] A) BCG Vaccine.
- [ ] B) Tetanus Toxoid and Rabies Vaccine (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis).
- [ ] C) Hepatitis B Vaccine.
- [ ] D) Oral Polio Vaccine (OPV).

**4. List two signs that would indicate the wound is becoming infected after a dog bite.**
- [ ] A) Increased pain, redness (erythema), warmth, or purulent drainage (pus).
- [ ] B) Tissue epithelialization and pale pink skin color.
- [ ] C) Reduced swelling and formation of a clean scab.
- [ ] D) Numbness that disappears within a few minutes.

**5. Why should traditional substances like butter or herbs NOT be applied to this wound?**
- [ ] A) They keep the wound too dry and cold.
- [ ] B) They introduct debris/bacteria, increase infection risk, and interfere with clinical assessment.
- [ ] C) They neutralize the rabies virus too quickly before the vaccine works.
- [ ] D) They are too expensive to use in rural healthcare settings.

### 🛑 GROUP 2: BURN WOUND MANAGEMENT

#### 📍 Scenario 3
A 4-year-old child is rushed to the clinic after spilling hot tea on her chest and upper abdomen. The skin is bright red, blistered, and the child is crying hysterically from severe pain.

**9. Based on the description, what is the depth/degree of this burn wound?**
- [ ] A) Superficial (First-degree) burn.
- [ ] B) Partial-thickness (Second-degree) burn.
- [ ] C) Full-thickness (Third-degree) burn.
- [ ] D) Fourth-degree deep tissue necrosis.

**10. What is the immediate first-aid action for a fresh thermal burn before dressing?**
- [ ] A) Break all the blisters immediately with a clean needle.
- [ ] B) Apply ice cubes directly to freeze the tissue.
- [ ] C) Cool the burn immediately with clean, cool running tap water for 10-20 minutes.
- [ ] D) Coat the area with toothpaste or heavy grease.

**11. Why is fluid resuscitation a priority in extensive burn management?**
- [ ] A) To wash out the topical ointment from the body.
- [ ] B) To prevent hypovolemic shock due to massive fluid loss from damaged skin barriers.
- [ ] C) To reduce the patient's urge to cry or express pain.
- [ ] D) To accelerate the immediate formation of an eschar.

**12. Why is a full-thickness burn often painless or less painful in the center?**
- [ ] A) The patient is in psychological denial.
- [ ] B) The heat destroys the local sensory nerve endings in the dermal layer.
- [ ] C) The charred tissue acts as a natural local anesthetic block.

- [ ] A) 9% (Arm only).
- [ ] B) 18% (Front torso only).
- [ ] C) 27% (Right arm 9% + Front torso 18%).
- [ ] D) 45% (Whole upper body).

**14. Mention two critical complications a nurse must monitor for in this severe burn patient.**
- [ ] A) Hypertension and excessive skin oiliness.
- [ ] B) Hypovolemic shock, systemic infection (sepsis), and compartment syndrome.

- [ ] D) High blood glucose and acute vitamin deficiency.

### 🛑 GROUP 3: SUTURING AND SURGICAL WOUND CARE

### 🛑 GROUP 5: HEALING PROCESS AND PATIENT FACTORS

#### 📍 Scenario 9
A 60-year-old patient with diabetes mellitus and a 20-year smoking history has a slow-healing foot ulcer. The nurse reviews factors affecting wound healing.

**25. How does diabetes mellitus delay the wound healing process?**
- [ ] A) It causes excessive tissue oxygenation and rapid blood flow.
- [ ] B) It impairs leukocyte function, compromises microvascular circulation, and delays collagen synthesis.
- [ ] C) It speeds up the inflammatory phase too quickly.
- [ ] D) It converts chronic wounds into acute primary intention wounds.

**26. How does nicotine/smoking interfere with tissue repair?**
- [ ] A) It acts as a vasodilator increasing local blood supply.
- [ ] B) It causes vasoconstriction, reduces oxygen-carrying capacity (carboxyhemoglobin), and leads to tissue hypoxia.
- [ ] C) It increases the production of red blood cells in the wound.
- [ ] D) It keeps the wound bed excessively moist.

**27. During the inflammatory phase of healing, what four local signs are expected around a wound?**
- [ ] A) Scarring, blanching, numbness, and cooling.
- [ ] B) Redness (rubor), heat (calor), swelling (tumor), and pain (dolor).
- [ ] C) Desiccation, maceration, undermining, and tunneling.
- [ ] D) Granulation, epithelialization, remodeling, and maturation.

**28. Which cells play the major role in phagocytosis and fighting infection during the inflammatory phase?**
- [ ] A) Erythrocytes and platelets.
- [ ] B) Leukocytes (Neutrophils and Macrophages).
- [ ] C) Fibroblasts and endothelial cells.
- [ ] D) Keratinocytes and melanocytes.

**29. The proliferative phase of healing involves the formation of which two key structures?**
- [ ] A) Thick white eschar and dry scabs.
- [ ] B) Granulation tissue and new blood vessels (angiogenesis).
- [ ] C) Deep sensory nerves and skeletal muscle fibers.
- [ ] D) Surgical sutures and medical staples.
